# Backlog citations drift out of date and the linter cannot re-point them

An item cites the code it is about by line — `path:NN:source text` — and
`queue.py lint` checks the fragment still sits within `--citation-window` lines
of where the row says it does. Ordinary refactors move those lines, and nothing
puts them back.

Measured 2026-09-10 over the 23 items at `a6fcd55`: 31 notes, 27 of them
`stale-citation`, spread across 8 items — Q35, Q36, Q38, Q39, Q40, Q43, Q44,
Q45. Of those, 26 name a line the fragment has moved to, drifting between 15
and 115 lines — every one outside the default window of 10. The 27th
(`base_run_failure()` in Q44) names a fragment `pr-sentinel-watch.sh` no longer
carries at all, so no re-point exists for it.

The store is not wrong and the gate is not failing. The cost is that the
pre-commit hook prints all 31 notes on **every** commit, so the 4 that a reader
has to act on arrive underneath 27 that nobody will. A signal at that ratio
stops being read, which is the same failure as having no linter.

Hand-repointing is the obvious move and it is a treadmill: the fragments are
correct until the next person touches those files, and the 27 come back. The
linter already knows both halves of the repair — it has the fragment text and
it found the line the text is now on, which is how it words the note.

## Proposal

Candidates, not a prescription.

- **`lint --fix`.** Rewrite the line number in place when the fragment is found
  exactly once elsewhere in the file, leave the note when it is ambiguous or
  gone. Turns 27 notes into a diff a reader can check, and the ambiguous cases
  are the ones worth a human anyway.
- **Cite without a line.** Keep `path:source text` and let the linter locate
  it every run. Removes the drift entirely rather than repairing it, at the
  cost of the citations no longer being clickable.
- **Fail rather than note.** `--strict stale-citation` already exists, so the
  gate could bind and force repair at the moment of breakage. Cheapest to
  turn on and the most disruptive: it makes an unrelated refactor's commit
  fail on nine items its author did not write.
